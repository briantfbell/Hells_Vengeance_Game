# Hell’s Bubbies — Suno soundtrack prompt book

Copy one complete prompt below into Suno for each cue. Use the available instrumental setting as well as the wording; leave lyrics empty. This is a production prompt list; its Suno tracks have not been generated or installed. The game now has an audio system with temporary original cues, volume controls, and file hooks. See COMBAT_AUDIO_AND_DEBTS.md.

The sound is dark chamber/orchestral fantasy with a restrained retro accent: roughly 90% acoustic/orchestral character and 10% chiptune color as an artistic target. No vocals, choir, humming, narration or spoken samples. Avoid modern EDM drops, overpowering sub-bass, long intros and incessant maximum-volume percussion. The player party serves an infernal regime; victory should sound proud, threatening or complicated rather than automatically righteous.

The recorded custom campaign takes priority over the published adventure path. This list follows Kintargo, Longacre, Kantaria, Senara, the capital intrigues and the later royal missions in the local timeline. Westcrown and the full published endgame are reserved scoring proposals, not claims that the table played those encounters. Hell’s Rebels events in the shared archive are not automatically part of this party’s story.

## Generate first

Start with 01, 04, 08, 12, 16, 17, 46–54 and 55. These cover the current game, near-term chapters and enough combat variation to avoid repetition. Generate two candidates per cue, then select by consistency and readability under dialogue. Make later-location cues as their chapters are built.

## Recurring musical ideas

- Party / imperial service: descending four-note minor figure followed by an unresolved rise. Lute or cello in quiet scenes, brass in battle.
- Glorious Reclamation: rising dignified horn / organ phrase. Let it sound sincerely noble even when the party opposes it.
- Infernal contracts: low repeating interval and dry harpsichord / bronze bell; instrumental, never chant.
- Friendship / rest: a warmer sparse version of the party contour, still with unresolved harmony.

Independent generations may not reproduce an exact melody. Choose one accepted title cue as the musical reference when you later arrange or revise the score; these verbal directions request a relationship, not guaranteed melodic continuity.

## Variation and playback plan

Keep tracks 46/47/48 in the routine pool, 49/50 in the occult pool, 51/52 in the beast/undead pool, 53/54 in the soldier pool, and 59/60 in the infernal pool. Avoid the previous cue when picking the next ordinary fight. Boss cues override the pool; reserve the final cue for the final confrontation. Keep victory/defeat stings separate so the loop does not determine encounter length.

For each accepted battle cue, request a second candidate with the same tempo/palette but a different motif and percussion pattern. Tag filenames by pool and version, for example `combat-street-b-v1`, rather than by one enemy so they can be reused without repeating every fight.

Ask for stable repetition, then audition the actual result: loop points still need listening and editing. Retain the complete generated master, choose a musically matching middle section, trim at phrase boundaries, and crossfade the seam if necessary. Target 60–120-second gameplay loops. Match perceived loudness between cues; make dialogue music quieter and reduce battle music while dice and effects sound. Check with a small mono speaker as well as headphones. Do not promise seamless loops from prompt wording alone.
